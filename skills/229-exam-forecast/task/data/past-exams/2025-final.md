## I. Case Analysis Questions (20 points each, 80 points total)

### Case 1 (about 450 characters; multi-link contractual chain)

In September 2024, restaurant chain brand H and ingredient supplier I signed an annual supply framework contract, agreeing that H would purchase no less than CNY 300K of frozen goods per month, with unit prices to be agreed monthly, and I responsible for cold-chain delivery. The contract's supplementary terms contained a clause printed in small print: "I bears no liability whatsoever for business losses caused by delivery delays." The person in charge on the H side had never read this supplementary provision. In January 2025, after H's store expansion plan was announced, I unilaterally notified H: "Due to rising raw material costs, supply prices will increase by 25% starting in February; otherwise supply will be stopped." H refused to accept this, and I therefore stopped supply starting February 3. H temporarily purchased replacement ingredients at a high price from Company J, incurring a price difference loss of CNY 120K, and, because 3 stores ran out of stock, suspended operations for 5 days, with estimated losses of CNY 90K. In March, H assigned its claims under the supply contract to affiliated company K and notified I; K immediately sued I for compensation.

Questions:
1. How should the validity of the disclaimer clause in the supplementary terms be determined? (6 points)
2. How should the nature of I's 25% price increase and supply stoppage be characterized? What remedy paths does H have? (8 points)
3. Can the CNY 120K price difference and the CNY 90K business loss in the losses be fully compensated? How is causation determined? (6 points)

### Case 2 (about 420 characters; fact-finding oriented)

On March 5, 2025, Company L and Company M were negotiating an office building renovation contract. On March 8, M sent a formal quotation, noting "the quotation is valid until March 20." On March 12, L called M to accept the quotation and asked that it be performed per the quotation; M said it had already withdrawn the quotation on March 10, on the grounds that "it is customary in the industry to quote this way, and one can back out at any time before signing the contract." On March 15, M signed a renovation contract with Company N for the same building at a price 10% higher. On March 18, L sent M a written letter reaffirming acceptance of the quotation. On March 22, M replied refusing to perform.

Questions:
1. M claims it withdrew the quotation on March 10; without a written record of the withdrawal, can this claim stand? Based on which facts in the materials do you make your determination? (8 points)
2. Was the contract between L and M formed on March 12? Why? (6 points)
3. After M signed a separate contract with N, what liability can L claim against M? How is the scope of compensation determined? (6 points)

### Case 3 (about 400 characters; lease and sublease)

In June 2024, P leased a shop to Q for a three-year term, and the contract stipulated "the shop may not be subleased without P's written consent." In April 2025, Q subleased the shop to R without consent and collected a deposit. In May 2025, the shop was damaged by water seepage from aging pipes on the floor above, and repairs shut the business for 10 days. R demanded that Q reduce the rent and compensate for the business suspension loss; Q, on the grounds that the seepage was caused by aging of P's building, demanded that P bear all repair and compensation costs. P, in turn, argued that it had fulfilled its duties as landlord, the seepage was due to property management negligence, unrelated to P, and Q's unauthorized sublease already constituted a breach, so P demanded rescission of the contract.

Questions:
1. Can P rescind the contract on the grounds of Q's unauthorized sublease? Analyze in light of the contract terms. (6 points)
2. Who should bear the business suspension loss from the seepage? How should the relationships among P, Q, and R be sorted out? (8 points)
3. If the contract is rescinded, how should the return of the deposit already paid by R be handled? (6 points)

### Case 4 (about 430 characters; pre-contractual negotiation and confidentiality)

Company S intended to acquire shares of Company T. The parties entered an exclusive negotiation stage and signed a confidentiality agreement, stipulating "during the negotiation period, neither party may contact third parties regarding this acquisition matter." After three months of negotiation, S unilaterally terminated the negotiations in February 2025, citing an internal strategic adjustment. In March of the same year, S began negotiating a similar acquisition with U, a direct competitor of T, and U obtained T's operating data during due diligence (the data source being a business plan T had proactively provided during the earlier negotiations). T asserted that S breached its exclusivity obligation and leaked its trade secrets, and should bear liability for compensation; and that S's termination of the negotiations constituted bad-faith negotiation.

Questions:
1. How should the determination of trade secrets and confidential information be approached in this case? (6 points)
2. Does S's unilateral termination of the negotiations constitute bad-faith negotiation? What is the standard for its determination? (8 points)
3. What role do the exclusivity clause and the confidentiality clause each play in the pre-contractual stage? What liability arises respectively when they are breached? (6 points)

## II. Essay Question (20 points)

In the context of an open-book exam, drawing on the rules of contract formation and validity in the cases above, discuss the manifestation of "the balance between freedom of contract and justice of contract" in China's contract law system, and give no fewer than three concrete institutional examples.
