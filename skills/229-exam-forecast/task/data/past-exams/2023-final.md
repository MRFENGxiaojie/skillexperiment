## III. Case Analysis Questions (25 points each, 50 points total)

### Case 1 (about 260 characters)

Company A, a furniture company, published a product page on its official website stating "Classic solid wood desk, promotional price CNY 1,999, this week only." B saw it, immediately placed an order, and completed payment. The next day, Company A notified B: "The promotional price was a system entry error; the actual price should be CNY 2,999. If you wish to purchase, please pay the difference; otherwise we will cancel the order." B refused to pay the difference and demanded delivery at CNY 1,999.

Questions:
1. Does this product page constitute an offer or an invitation to treat? Why? (8 points)
2. Can Company A unilaterally cancel the order? What remedies can B claim? (9 points)
3. If Company A invokes the defense that "the fine print at the bottom of the product page states: The company reserves the right to modify prices," is this clause valid? Please analyze it in light of the rules on standard terms. (8 points)

### Case 2 (about 240 characters)

C was viewing a property at Company D's sales office. A salesperson verbally promised, "Buying an apartment comes with a CNY 30K renovation subsidy; putting it in the contract is no problem." Relying on this, C gave up another property and signed the contract with Company D, but the contract text contained no renovation subsidy clause. After the apartment was delivered, C demanded that the subsidy be honored. Company D said the contract did not stipulate it, and the verbal promise was not binding.

Questions:
1. Can C claim culpa in contrahendo liability with respect to the renovation subsidy? Are the constituent elements satisfied? (13 points)
2. If a claim for breach of contract is asserted, can it succeed? Why? (6 points)
3. What insight does this case give you into handling the "contractually unstated but verbally promised" issue? (6 points)
