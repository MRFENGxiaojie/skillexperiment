# Client interview notes - tenant residential heating dispute case

- Interview date: 2026-03-18
- Interviewer: Law school legal aid clinic student (notetaker: Alex Lin)
- Client: Maria Chen (tenant, living with one minor child)

## Case background

- Rental property: 1324 Maple Avenue, Apartment 3B, Springfield
- Landlord/owner: Rogers Property Management LLC (managed directly by the owner)
- Lease: lease began on 2024-09-01 for 1 year; renewed on 2025-09-01 for 1 year (through 2026-08-31), monthly rent $1,450, security deposit $1,450

## Event timeline (as stated by the client)

- 2026-01-15: The apartment's heating system failed, and the indoor temperature dropped to about 10°C; the client reported the issue by phone
- 2026-01-16: The client reported the issue again by email; the landlord replied "will arrange it as soon as possible"
- 2026-01-20: Not fixed; the client contacted the landlord a third time; the landlord said "parts need to be ordered"
- 2026-01-27: The client contacted the landlord a fourth time; the landlord replied verbally "I'm on it", but still no progress
- 2026-01-30: The client bought two electric space heaters herself (total $340), and the indoor temperature barely stayed at about 14°C
- 2026-02-02: The client urged again; the landlord only arranged an on-site inspection (February 8) and did not actually fix the issue
- 2026-02-05: The client notified the landlord in writing (email): if the heating was not repaired by February 12, she would move out and terminate the lease
- 2026-02-10: The heating was still not repaired
- 2026-02-12: The client moved out with her child, returned the keys, and temporarily stayed at her mother's home
- The client stopped paying rent as of 2026-02-01
- 2026-03-05: The landlord's lawyer sent a letter (see landlord-letter.pdf), claiming rent arrears of $4,350 plus penalties and threatening litigation

## Client's demands

- Not to bear rent after February 1
- To get the $1,450 security deposit back
- To claim losses during the heating outage: electric heaters $340, moving expenses $480, and related expenses for the new residence
- Willing to settle, but the bottom line is not to pay rent after 2026-02-12

## Evidence in the client's possession

- Printouts of repair request emails (2026-01-16, 01-20, 01-27, 02-02, 02-05)
- Landlord's on-site inspection record (2026-02-08, not repaired)
- Receipts for the electric heaters (2026-01-30)
- Copy of the lease (see lease.pdf)
- Landlord's lawyer's letter (see landlord-letter.pdf)

## Preliminary legal issues (to be researched)

- Whether the landlord's failure to maintain heating constitutes breach / violation of the implied warranty of habitability
- Whether it constitutes constructive eviction
- Calculation of and defense to rent abatement
- The tenant's duty to mitigate and whether moving out was reasonable
- Whether the landlord's counterclaim for unpaid rent can be established
