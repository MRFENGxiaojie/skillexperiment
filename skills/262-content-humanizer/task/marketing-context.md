# Brand tone guide (marketing-context)

## Who we are
The website targets customer-service team leads / customer-service supervisors (operations leads at mid-sized companies). The articles are for the acquisition blog; readers will skim them quickly on both phones and computers.

## Tone principles
1. Direct and conversational: speak like a knowledgeable colleague, not like a consulting firm reading a report. You may ask readers questions; you may ask and answer yourself.
2. Vary sentence length: long sentences explain principles; short sentences deliver conclusions and numbers. Three or more consecutive sentences of the same length count as failing.
3. Use adjectives and degree words such as "very / extremely / significantly" sparingly; let facts and numbers speak.
4. First person ("we", "I") is allowed; addressing the reader as "you" is allowed.
5. Keep paragraphs short: no paragraph longer than 4 lines.
6. All data uses Arabic numerals, and units are written out clearly (% keep one decimal place).

## Banned-words list (their appearance counts as machine-made)
empower / empowerment; lever / grip; closed loop; accumulate; deep-dive; land (abstract usage); craft / build; boost; as everyone knows; in conclusion; in summary; undoubtedly; in today's (XX) era; it is worth noting; not only... but also (in combination); one-stop; seamless; significantly improve; premium; efficient (when piled up alone); let us join hands; co-create the future; fast-paced; digital wave; keep up with the times; endless potential; ultimate experience.

## Negative sentence-pattern examples (avoid)
- "In today's fast-paced digital era, ... has become an important topic."
- "First, ... Second, ... Third, ... Finally, ..." (the article structure must not use ladder-style parallel constructions)
- "In conclusion, ... is an irreversible trend."

## Positive sentence-pattern examples (reference)
- "How many times a day does a support team answer 'how do I issue an invoice'? Count, and you'll know it's time to automate."
- "Automation takes the repetitive questions off your hands; humans only touch the genuinely tricky ones."
- "Don't think of automation as a layoff tool. After most teams finish, headcount doesn't shrink — but the quality of the scripts goes up."

## Content red lines (not to be changed)
- All data, cases, and percentages in the text must be preserved as-is; changing the values is forbidden, and adding any new data is forbidden.
- No new cases or company names may be added.
- What changes is wording and rhythm, not the facts.
