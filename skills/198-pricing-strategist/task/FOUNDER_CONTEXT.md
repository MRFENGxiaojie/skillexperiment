## Product Positioning

- Product: an online collaborative document tool for small and mid-size teams (like a lightweight Notion): pages/documents, block editing, multi-user real-time collaboration, knowledge base/spaces, simple permissions, comments and @mentions, export.
- Form: Web app + desktop (Electron) + mobile (iOS/Android). The core selling point is "works out of the box, fast to learn, inexpensive" — positioned as a "lightweight alternative" to Notion and Feishu, not competing on feature breadth.
- Differentiation: great Chinese-language experience, fast access in China, template marketplace; light integrations with existing team tools (WeCom/DingTalk).

## Target Customer Profile

- Primary audience: small/mid teams of 20–200 people (startups, studios, consulting/design/marketing teams, university research groups).
- Decision maker: team lead/founder/COO or tech lead, price-sensitive, dislikes complex per-seat billing and hidden fees.
- Secondary audience: individual users (knowledge management, notes).

## Current Paid Status

- Live for 8 months, ~32K registered users, ~11K daily active users.
- Free-tier users are 96% of the base; ~200 teams are paying (150 of them annual).
- Currently a single paid tier (an internal stopgap: ¥20/mo/user, functionally almost identical to free), no pricing strategy to speak of.
- The free tier has no limits (unlimited collaborators, unlimited spaces), which weakens the incentive to pay.

## Stage and Revenue Goals

- Stage: Pre-A, shifting from "free acquisition" to commercialization validation.
- Revenue goal: ¥500K MRR within 6 months; paid conversion rate up from the current 0.6% to over 2%.
- Team of 22; no dedicated commercialization lead; the founder/CEO owns pricing decisions directly.

## Questions to Discuss (internal points of disagreement)

1. Which features/usage limits should the free tier gate (team is split between "keep the free tier generous" and "must create a reason to pay").
2. Per-seat vs. a flat team price (small/mid teams are sensitive about per-seat).
3. Whether to build a paid individual plan (as a funnel entry for conversion).
4. The trade-off between an annual discount and a "free forever" promise.
