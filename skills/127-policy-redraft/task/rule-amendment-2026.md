# Regulation (PPI) 2026 — Amendments to the Protection of Personal Information

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Citation** | Regulation No. 2026/014 on the Protection of Personal Information (the "Regulation") |
| **Issuing authority** | [Name of competent regulatory authority — the Data Protection Regulator for the company's principal market] |
| **Published** | 2026-03-02 |
| **Effective date** | 2026-06-01 |
| **Applies to** | All controllers and processors that collect, use, or otherwise process personal information in connection with the regulated activity |
| **Replaces** | The narrower definition of "Personal Information" previously applied by the company under its Personal Information Protection Policy |

This document sets out the provisions of the 2026 amendment that are relevant
to the company's personal information policies. Where this Regulation and a
company policy differ, the Regulation governs from its effective date.

---

## Article 1 — Effective date

This Regulation enters into force and takes effect on **2026-06-01**.
Controllers are required to bring their policies, notices, and processing
practices into compliance by that date.

## Article 2 — Definition of "Personal Information"

### 2.1 General definition

For the purposes of this Regulation, **"Personal Information"** means any
information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (a "data
subject"). A natural person is identifiable, directly or indirectly, in
particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification
number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors
specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural,
or social identity of that person.

### 2.2 Scope of the definition

The definition in Article 2.1 expressly includes:

- **Pseudonymized data** — information from which direct identifiers have been
  replaced or removed, where a key or other means could re-associate the data
  with a natural person. Pseudonymized data remains Personal Information;
- **Behavioral and derived data** — information generated from a person's
  behavior, interactions, preferences, or use of products and services,
  including inferences, profiling outputs, and analytics; and
- **Data combined from multiple sources** — information that identifies or
  makes identifiable a natural person only when aggregated or cross-referenced
  across records, systems, or datasets.

### 2.3 No carve-out for unstructured storage

The definition in Article 2.1 applies regardless of the medium or format in
which the information is held. There is no exclusion for information that is
not stored in a structured system; information in free-form documents, emails,
audio or video recordings, and other unstructured sources is within scope
where it relates to an identified or identifiable natural person.

### 2.4 Exclusion

Only information that is genuinely and irreversibly aggregated or
de-identified — such that no reasonable means could re-associate it with a
natural person — falls outside the definition.

## Article 3 — Lawful bases and transparency

Processing of Personal Information is lawful only where a valid lawful basis
applies. Controllers must provide notice of the categories of Personal
Information processed, the purposes of processing, and the lawful basis relied
upon, in plain and accessible language.

## Article 4 — Data minimization and purpose limitation

Controllers may collect and process Personal Information only to the extent
that it is adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to
the purposes for which it is processed. Personal Information may not be further
processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes.

---

## Article 5 — Storage limitation and deletion timelines

### 5.1 Storage limitation

Personal Information must be kept in a form which permits identification of
data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which it is
processed.

### 5.2 Deletion on request

Upon a verified request from the data subject, the controller must erase the
relevant Personal Information without undue delay, and in any event **within
30 calendar days** of the request, subject to limited statutory exceptions.

### 5.3 Deletion at end of retention

When a retention period documented in the controller's records-retention
schedule expires, the controller must delete the Personal Information **within
60 calendar days** and document the deletion.

---

## Article 6 — Breach notification

### 6.1 Notification to the authority

A Personal Information Breach must be notified to the competent regulatory
authority **within 72 hours** of the controller becoming aware of it, unless
the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of data
subjects.

### 6.2 Notification to data subjects

Where the breach is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms
of data subjects, the controller must notify the affected data subjects
**without undue delay** and provide the information required by Article 6.3.

### 6.3 Contents of notification

The notification must describe the nature of the breach, the categories and
approximate number of data subjects and records concerned, the likely
consequences, and the measures taken or proposed to address the breach and
mitigate its adverse effects.

---

## Article 7 — Records of processing

Controllers must maintain a record of processing activities describing the
categories of Personal Information processed, the purposes of processing, the
categories of recipients, the retention periods, and the technical and
organizational measures applied.

## Article 8 — Transitional provision

Processing practices, internal policies, and notices that are inconsistent with
this Regulation must be brought into compliance by the effective date in
Article 1. Nothing in this Regulation requires the re-collection of Personal
Information already lawfully held.
