# Post Templates

Complete post skeletons for the five most common post types. Every template starts with a hook (see Hook Formulas in SKILL.md) and ends with a CTA. Fill the bracketed fields with the user's content; keep the structure.

---

## 1. Announcement Template

When: new product, feature, milestone, offer.

```
[Hook — surprise, result, or contrarian take]

[One sentence: what's new]

[What it does / why it matters — 2-3 bullets]
- [Benefit 1]
- [Benefit 2]
- [Benefit 3]

[Proof or context — numbers, screenshots, or a short story]

CTA: [Try it / Read more / Sign up] — [link or "link in comments"]
```

**Example (LinkedIn):**

> We killed our 4-step onboarding. New users now hit value in under 60 seconds.
>
> What changed:
> - 1 field instead of 9
> - Instant demo data instead of empty states
> - A 30-second "aha" screen instead of a tutorial
>
> The metric that matters: activation went from 38% to 61% in the first two weeks.
>
> Try it: [link]

## 2. Educational Template

When: teaching something your audience struggles with.

```
[Hook — how-to, number list, or mistake framing]

[The mistake or the gap — what most people do wrong]

[The fix — 3-5 steps or one framework]
1. [Step 1]
2. [Step 2]
3. [Step 3]

[What happens when they do it right — result or before/after]

CTA: [Save this for later / Follow for part 2]
```

**Example (Twitter/X thread):**

> The #1 reason your content gets no engagement isn't the algorithm.
>
> It's the first line. If it doesn't stop the scroll, nothing else matters.
>
> Here's a 3-step hook framework:
> 1. Name the thing everyone feels ("we all skip intros")
> 2. Add the twist ("the intro is the post")
> 3. Deliver the payoff ("say the thing in line one")
>
> Try it on your next post.

## 3. Story Template

When: building personal connection or trust.

```
[Hook — unexpected event or failure]

[Scene 1: what happened — time, place, detail]

[Scene 2: the tension — the problem or near-miss]

[Scene 3: the resolution — what you learned or did]

[The takeaway — one line that applies to the reader]

CTA: [Question to the audience / Share your version]
```

**Example (Instagram caption + carousel):**

> I almost quit 3 months in. Here's what changed my mind.
>
> [Carousel: 1 — the moment it felt hopeless; 2 — the conversation that reframed it; 3 — the one metric I started watching; 4 — what I'd tell my past self]
>
> If you're early in the grind: the first 90 days are the worst data you'll ever have. Don't quit on it.

## 4. Opinion Template

When: taking a position, building a point of view.

```
[Hook — unpopular opinion / contrarian claim]

[The position — one clear sentence]

[Why — 2-3 reasons, each with an example]

[The counterargument — name it, then answer it]

[What you'd do instead]

CTA: [Do you agree? / What's your take?]
```

**Example (LinkedIn):**

> Unpopular opinion: posting daily is hurting your brand.
>
> Publishing 7 mediocre posts a week teaches your audience to scroll past you.
>
> Why:
> - Consistency ≠ value. One strong insight beats seven recycled ones.
> - The algorithm rewards completion, not volume — a post that gets 200 reads in the first hour beats one that lingers at 12.
> - You're trading strategy time for posting time.
>
> "But the gurus say daily..." The gurus are selling posting schedules, not results.
>
> Try: 2-3 thoughtful posts a week for 60 days. Compare the engagement rate.

## 5. Engagement Template

When: starting conversations, building community.

```
[Hook — question, poll, or open loop]

[Context — one or two sentences so the question makes sense]

[The question — specific, answerable in 30 seconds]

[Optional: your own answer first — lowers the barrier]

CTA: [Reply with X / Vote in the poll]
```

**Example (LinkedIn):**

> What's the most overrated "growth hack" you keep seeing?
>
> Mine: "post 3x a day for 90 days." If the content is noise, the algorithm punishes it and your audience leaves.
>
> What's yours? Reply with one.

## Template Selection Rules

| Goal | Template |
|------|----------|
| Launch / news | Announcement |
| Teach | Educational |
| Build trust | Story |
| Build authority | Opinion |
| Community / feedback | Engagement |

Rules that apply to all five:

1. Hook is always the first element — never deliver copy body without it (see SKILL.md).
2. One post = one job. Announcement posts don't teach; opinion posts don't announce.
3. Every template ends with a CTA. No CTA, no post.
4. Adapt length and formatting to the platform (see Platform Quick Reference in SKILL.md).
5. Numbers in posts must come from the user — never invent statistics.
