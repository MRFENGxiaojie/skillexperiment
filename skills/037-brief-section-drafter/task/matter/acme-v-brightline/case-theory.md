## Theory (one paragraph)

The parties' commercial relationship is governed by the Supply Agreement dated
2025-12-01, which fixes payment at net-30 days and allows amendment only by a written
instrument signed by authorized representatives of both parties (§7.2). Brightline
now claims that a February 2026 email exchange (in which Acme's sales director said
Acme "can be flexible on timing") modified the payment terms to net-60. No such
amendment exists or was ever signed; §7.2 forecloses the claim. Brightline owes
$48,500.00 on invoices 10258, 10264, and 10279, plus interest under §4.3. The email
exchange at most shows a willingness to discuss — not a modification. Brightline's
own conduct (its 2026-02-09 purchase approval form, signed by its procurement
director Zhang Wei, confirming original pricing and net-30 terms) corroborates that
no modification was ever agreed.

## Pivot fact

**Zhang Wei (Brightline's Director of Procurement) signed the 2026-02-09 purchase
approval form (Brightline Bates BL-000214) which confirms the BL-8841 order at
original pricing with net-30 terms — nine days after Brightline's claimed
"modification."** If Zhang Wei confirms that approval form at deposition, Brightline's
modification defense collapses: no executive can claim the parties changed terms on
2026-02-03 while the company's own procurement approval of the same order, signed
2026-02-09, recorded the original terms.

## Key facts for us

1. Supply Agreement 2025-12-01; §4.2 (net-30), §4.3 (interest), §7.2 (written amendment required).
2. Invoices 10258 ($13,200), 10264 ($18,900), 10279 ($16,400) — all due and unpaid since March-April 2026; total $48,500.
3. No written amendment to the Agreement was ever executed (no counterpart; no signature page) — confirmed by our records and Brightline's production to date.
4. Brightline paid invoice 10231 (2026-02-02) under net-30 terms — conduct consistent with original terms, not modified terms.
5. 2026-02-09 approval form (BL-000214) signed by Zhang Wei recording original price and net-30.
6. Brightline's 2026-04-05 email (Zhang Wei to J. Park) is the first document asserting "60-day terms" — six weeks after the claimed modification date.

## Key facts against us (must be addressed, not ignored)

1. 2026-02-03 email chain: Acme sales director J. Park wrote "we can be flexible on
   timing given this year's volume" — Brightline reads this as agreement to net-60.
   (Our response: "flexible" is a discussion posture, not a term; no price/term was
   stated; §7.2 requires a signed writing regardless; and the 02-09 approval form
   undercuts any claim that a modification was agreed.)
2. Acme withheld the June 2026 order (BL-8842) without prior notice — Brightline may
   characterize this as a breach/retaliation. (Our response: withholding is not the
   subject of this motion; and if argued, cure language of §9.3 was satisfied by
   written notice 2026-05-15.)
3. No written "no-modification" confirmation exists from February 2026 — the record
   is silence between 02-03 and 04-05. (Address by pointing to the 02-09 approval
   form and the 02-02 payment.)

## Issues on summary judgment (this motion)

1. Whether the February 2026 email exchange effected a modification of §4.2
   (our position: no — §7.2 writing-and-signature requirement; no genuine dispute).
2. Whether Brightline's claimed modification defense fails as a matter of law.
3. Whether interest under §4.3 runs from each invoice's due date.
