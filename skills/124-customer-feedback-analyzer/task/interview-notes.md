# Key-Account Interview Notes — July 2026

Four interviews were held during the last week of July with our most engaged accounts. All quotes are paraphrased from the call notes and are shared with the account's permission.

---

## 1. BrightMedia Agency (devin@brightmedia.io)
- **Company:** BrightMedia, a 40-person digital agency in Toronto.
- **Interviewee role:** Head of Operations.
- **What they said:**
  - "We live in the tool all day. The single biggest quality-of-life fix for our team would be dark mode — three designers asked about it on the first day."
  - "When we onboarded five new contractors last month, two couldn't find their workspace because the invite email was sitting in spam."
- **Key takeaways:**
  - Dark mode is a top-of-mind request, not a nice-to-have, for this account.
  - Email deliverability (invite/spam) directly affects onboarding success and first-week churn risk.
  - They would happily become a design-partner account to test dark mode before launch.

---

## 2. Hanami Games (kenji.t@hanami.gg)
- **Company:** Hanami Games, a 25-person game studio in Tokyo; runs ~30 concurrent projects.
- **Interviewee role:** Producer / head of delivery.
- **What they said:**
  - "In the afternoon our reports just time out. I had to tell our PMs to export before 2pm, which is ridiculous."
  - "We are swimming in tasks. Search is basically unusable once we pass five thousand items, so people stop trying to find things."
- **Key takeaways:**
  - Performance is an account-level blocker; reliability incidents (afternoon timeouts) erode trust fast.
  - Search over many projects is a genuine scale problem, not a perception issue.
  - This account will likely churn if performance is not addressed within two release cycles.

---

## 3. Café Internacional (maria.santos@cafeinternacional.com.br)
- **Company:** Café Internacional, a 60-person retail group in São Paulo, now rolling the tool out to all stores.
- **Interviewee role:** Digital transformation lead.
- **What they said:**
  - "We pay in BRL but the invoices are in USD, and the tax is never right. Accounting keeps chasing me about it."
  - "The onboarding doc does not mention how to invite a team. For a tool sold to managers, that is the first thing they want to do."
- **Key takeaways:**
  - Localized billing (currency + local tax) matters for expanding international accounts.
  - First-run experience is underselling the core "invite your team" action; template projects would shorten time-to-value.

---

## 4. Compass Labs (ian@compasslabs.com)
- **Company:** Compass Labs, a 120-person engineering consultancy in Berlin; the account with the most seats.
- **Interviewee role:** Engineering manager.
- **What they said:**
  - "We evaluate tools on how quickly a new project can go from idea to a working board. Right now that means building templates by hand for a day."
  - "We'd pay more for a plan with per-user workload limits so leads can see who is over capacity."
- **Key takeaways:**
  - Powerful accounts want project templates and workload visibility — both are expansion/pricing levers.
  - Long-term, this account wants an admin/analytics tier; good candidate for a beta of workload features.
