# Sales Motion Design Guide

Use when the chosen motion is sales-led or hybrid (sales-assist on top of PLG). The goal is a repeatable path from inbound/outbound interest to a closed deal with measurable handoffs.

## When sales applies

- ACV high enough that a rep's time is cheaper than leaving conversion to self-serve (typically > $5K-$25K depending on margin)
- Complex procurement: security review, legal, procurement, multi-stakeholder buying
- Custom requirements: integrations, onboarding, negotiated contracts

## Roles and handoffs

| Stage | Owner | Output | Handoff trigger |
|-------|-------|--------|-----------------|
| Inbound/outbound | Marketing/SDR | Qualified lead | Fit + intent criteria met |
| Qualification | SDR | Meeting booked | Sales-qualified signal |
| Demo/evaluation | AE | Proposal | Requirements confirmed |
| Procurement | AE + legal | Contract | Security/procurement cleared |
| Onboarding | CS | Activated customer | Contract signed |

## Routing rules

- Route by segment: ICP tier, ACV, region, and source (PQL vs inbound vs outbound)
- Define disqualifiers explicitly (students, competitors, tiny companies, unsupported geo)
- One owner per deal; no silent handoffs

## SLAs

- First touch on high-intent lead: < 24 hours
- Proposal follow-up: < 48 hours
- Every SLA has a fallback (escalation to a named person)

## Pipeline metrics

- Meetings booked per rep/week
- Demo-to-proposal conversion
- Win rate by segment and source
- Sales cycle length (by ACV band)
- Pipeline coverage (pipeline / target)
- Forecast accuracy (committed vs closed)

## Common pitfalls

- Handoffs without a handoff criteria (deals stall in the gap)
- SLAs without owners (everyone assumes someone else)
- Sales and marketing measuring different funnels (one source of truth required)
- Over-servicing low-ACV deals with enterprise process
