# Effects of Liraglutide Combined with Metformin on Glycemic Variability and Cardiovascular Risk Markers in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Authors**: Liu Fang¹, Zhao Lei², Sun Xiaomei¹, Wang Jianjun¹
1. Department of Endocrinology, Second Affiliated Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine; 2. Department of Cardiology, Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University

## Abstract

Objective: To evaluate the effects of liraglutide combined with metformin (L+M group) versus metformin alone (M group) on glycemic variability (GV) and cardiovascular risk markers in patients with type 2 diabetes. Methods: 180 patients with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes were randomized 1:1 into two groups and treated for 24 weeks. The primary endpoint was the coefficient of variation (CV) from continuous glucose monitoring (CGM); secondary endpoints included HOMA-IR, hs-CRP, carotid intima-media thickness (IMT), and the incidence of hypoglycemia. Results: After 24 weeks of treatment, CV in the L+M group decreased from a baseline of 31.4% (SD 5.2%) to 24.6% (SD 4.8%), and in the M group from 30.9% (SD 5.6%) to 28.3% (SD 5.1%), with a statistically significant between-group difference (P = 0.008). The between-group difference for HOMA-IR was -0.9 (95% CI -1.5 to -0.3, P = 0.004); for hs-CRP it was -0.8 mg/L (95% CI -1.4 to -0.2, P = 0.011). Hypoglycemia incidence was 8.9% and 7.8% (P = 0.79). Conclusion: Adding liraglutide to metformin significantly improves glycemic variability and some cardiovascular risk markers in patients with type 2 diabetes without increasing hypoglycemia risk — the net clinical benefit of this combination strategy is clear.

**Keywords**: liraglutide; metformin; glycemic variability; randomized controlled trial; type 2 diabetes

## 1. Introduction

Glycemic variability is an independent risk factor for diabetic complications apart from HbA1c [1-3]. Growing evidence suggests that fluctuating hyperglycemia harms endothelial function even more than sustained hyperglycemia [4]. It is worth noting that existing studies mostly focus on the HbA1c target-attainment rate, while paying clearly insufficient attention to the dimension of glycemic fluctuation — even though the latter is more directly related to oxidative stress and endothelial injury [4-5]. As a GLP-1 receptor agonist, liraglutide, while lowering HbA1c, has shown potential to improve glycemic stability [5]. However, high-quality randomized controlled evidence on its effect on glycemic variability when combined with metformin is still lacking.

In summary, this study aims to answer the following question: can adding liraglutide to metformin-based treatment significantly improve glycemic variability and lower cardiovascular risk marker levels in patients with type 2 diabetes within 24 weeks?

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Study design and participants

This is a single-center, open-label, randomized controlled trial (registration no. ChiCTR-2300088812). Inclusion criteria: age 30-70 years; HbA1c 7.5%-10.5%; metformin monotherapy for ≥ 3 months with a stable dose. Exclusion criteria: type 1 diabetes, severe hepatic or renal impairment, pregnancy or lactation, prior GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy. A total of 180 patients were enrolled and randomized into the L+M group (n = 90) and the M group (n = 90).

### 2.2 Intervention and follow-up

L+M group: liraglutide added to metformin (1.5-2.0 g/d), starting at 0.6 mg/d, titrated up by 0.6 mg weekly to a maintenance dose of 1.8 mg/d within 4 weeks. The M group continued their original metformin dose. Both groups received identical dietary and exercise guidance. CGM was worn for 72 hours at baseline and at week 24 — with routine diet and daily routine maintained during this period — and fasting blood samples were collected concurrently.

### 2.3 Statistical methods

Per-protocol (PP) analysis served as a sensitivity analysis; the primary analysis used intention-to-treat (ITT). Between-group comparisons used analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) adjusted for baseline values and age. Glycemic variability measures included CV, mean amplitude of glycemic excursions (MAGE), and standard deviation (SD). Hypoglycemia was defined as blood glucose < 3.9 mmol/L.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Baseline characteristics

Baseline characteristics were balanced between the two groups (all P > 0.05). Mean age 53.6 years (SD 9.8), 58.3% male, baseline HbA1c 8.9% (SD 0.8%), baseline CV 31.2% (SD 5.4%).

### 3.2 Primary endpoint

At week 24, CV decreased by 6.8 percentage points in the L+M group and by 2.6 percentage points in the M group — the adjusted between-group difference was -4.1 percentage points (95% CI -7.1 to -1.1, P = 0.008), with an effect size of Cohen's d = 0.42. Notably, the between-group difference in MAGE was -0.9 mmol/L (95% CI -1.6 to -0.2, P = 0.015), indicating that liraglutide also significantly improved within-day fluctuation.

### 3.3 Secondary endpoints

HOMA-IR: L+M group decreased from 4.2 (SD 1.3) to 2.8 (SD 1.0); M group from 4.1 (SD 1.2) to 3.7 (SD 1.1); between-group difference -0.9 (95% CI -1.5 to -0.3, P = 0.004). hs-CRP between-group difference -0.8 mg/L (95% CI -1.4 to -0.2, P = 0.011). IMT between-group difference -0.03 mm (95% CI -0.08 to 0.02, P = 0.24) — did not reach statistical significance. In conclusion, adding liraglutide produced significant improvements in both the insulin-resistance and inflammatory-marker dimensions.

### 3.4 Safety

The incidence of nausea (21.1% vs 8.9%) and diarrhea (14.4% vs 6.7%) was higher in the L+M group than in the M group, mostly mild to moderate and transient — largely resolving on their own within 4 weeks. No severe hypoglycemia or pancreatitis occurred in either group.

## 4. Discussion

The results of this study show that liraglutide combined with metformin can significantly improve glycemic variability in patients with type 2 diabetes within 24 weeks, and this effect remains robust after adjusting for baseline HbA1c. This finding has clear clinical significance — glycemic fluctuation has been confirmed to be closely related to oxidative stress, vascular endothelial dysfunction, and the progression of diabetic complications [1,3-4]. Mechanistically, GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce glycemic fluctuation through glucose-dependent glucose-lowering pathways [6], which is consistent with the concurrent decrease we observed in both CV and MAGE. Compared with previous studies [7-8], the unique features of this study are setting the CGM coefficient of variation as the pre-specified primary endpoint and incorporating a combined assessment of cardiovascular risk markers.

Notably, this study has the following limitations: (1) the open-label design may introduce measurement bias, but the primary endpoint is based on objective CGM data, limiting the impact; (2) the single-center sample size is limited; (3) the 24-week follow-up cannot answer the question of long-term cardiovascular benefit. Nevertheless, the strength of the evidence in this study is sufficient to support incorporating glycemic fluctuation into glycemic management strategies.

## 5. Conclusion

Adding liraglutide to metformin for 24 weeks significantly improves glycemic variability in patients with type 2 diabetes and lowers HOMA-IR and hs-CRP levels without increasing hypoglycemia risk. This combination can serve as an effective strategy for improving glycemic fluctuation and cardiovascular risk management.
